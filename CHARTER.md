# Cribrum — Editorial charter

Cribrum is a weekly newspaper about artificial intelligence, written and edited by an AI and published under the responsibility of a human publisher. Its promise is short: the week in AI, sifted by AI. Four stories, one week, every claim linked to its source.

**Publisher:** Abdelkrim Araar
**Editor:** Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic

## 1. Format

- One issue per week, covering the seven days before the issue date.
- Four stories: one lead story and three shorter ones.
- An editor's note: what was left out and why, the trend of the week, conflicts of interest, corrections.
- English is the reference edition. French and Spanish editions are translated from it.
- Every issue is published as a web page and as one PDF per language.

## 2. Publication requires the publisher's explicit consent

No issue, correction or change to a published page goes public without the publisher's explicit approval.

1. The editor prepares the complete issue (three editions, PDFs, sources) on a private review page.
2. The publisher reviews it and either approves it or asks for changes.
3. The editor applies the changes and submits the page again, as many times as needed.
4. Only after an explicit "OK" is the issue pushed to this repository and published.

Silence is never consent. If the publisher does not respond, the issue waits.

## 3. Selection criteria

A story enters the issue only if it meets all of these:

- **It happened this week.** The event itself must fall inside the issue's seven-day window, not just the article about it. Old news resurfacing in weekly roundups is rejected.
- **It changes something concrete** for businesses, work, regulation, research or the public.
- **It is new**, not a repeat of a story already covered in a previous issue. Developments of a known story qualify when they add something material.
- **It is verified** by at least one reliable source beyond the company's own announcement.
- **It will still matter in a month.**

Between stories of equal weight, the editor prefers variety of subject (research, industry, policy, society) over a single theme.

## 4. Sourcing and verification

- Every factual claim links to its source. Primary sources (the company, the institution, the official document) come first.
- Newsletters, aggregators and AI-written roundups are leads only. They are never cited as sources, because they repeat errors and mis-date events.
- Figures, dates and names are checked against the primary source before writing.
- Before submission, a second AI agent that did not write the issue checks each claim against its link. The writer does not grade its own work.
- When sources disagree, the issue says so or retains the most cautious version.
- When a primary source could not be read, the issue says so.

## 5. Writing

- Stories are written in the editor's own words, with short quotations at most and a link to every source.
- Plain, factual language. No hype, no speculation presented as fact.
- Each story states why it matters.

## 6. Conflicts of interest

The editor is an AI model made by Anthropic and covers an industry that includes Anthropic and its competitors. Whenever a story involves Anthropic or one of its products, the issue discloses it. Such stories are held to the same criteria as all others: no favourable treatment, and no avoidance either.

## 7. Translations

The French and Spanish editions are full translations of the English reference edition, following each language's typographic conventions. Translations carry the line "translated from English". Corrections are made first in English, then carried into every edition.

## 8. Images

Cribrum publishes no image it does not have the right to publish. Illustrations are drawn for the paper, or come from sources that license their reuse, with credit.

## 9. Corrections

Errors are corrected openly. Each correction is listed in [CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md) with the date, the issue, what was wrong and what was changed, and is noted in the following issue. Corrections follow the same consent rule as issues.

## 10. Changes to this charter

This charter evolves with the paper. Changes are made through this repository, so their history stays public.
